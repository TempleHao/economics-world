# Economics World — Digital Money, Web3 & DeFi Track

## Positioning

This track is a core future-finance world, not a crypto trading course.

Learning objective:
1. Understand how money, ledgers, settlement, ownership and financial intermediation evolved into programmable networks.
2. Understand the economic mechanisms behind stablecoins, CBDCs, tokenised deposits, DeFi and tokenised assets.
3. Distinguish technology, balance-sheet structure, legal claim, settlement asset and business model.
4. Learn from failed experiments rather than telling a linear “technology always wins” story.
5. Build the ability to evaluate real commercial opportunities in cross-border payments, treasury, banking and capital markets.

## Monetary architecture comparison

Every major form of digital money should be compared using the same questions:

1. Whose liability is it?
2. What asset backs it?
3. Who can issue it?
4. How is par convertibility maintained?
5. Where does settlement finality occur?
6. Who provides elasticity/liquidity in stress?
7. What happens in a run?
8. How does AML/KYC work?
9. Who earns the economics?
10. What happens if the technology layer fails?

## Business opportunity framework

Every opportunity must be rated:
- A — already commercial
- B — live pilots / early scaling
- C — plausible 3–5 year opportunity
- D — speculative / high uncertainty

For every opportunity answer:
1. Customer
2. Pain point
3. Why existing rails are inadequate
4. What blockchain/tokenisation actually changes
5. Revenue model
6. Balance-sheet or liquidity need
7. Licensing/compliance burden
8. Technology dependency
9. Competitive moat
10. Failure mode

### Opportunity 1 — Stablecoin orchestration
Status: A/B
Products:
- fiat ↔ stablecoin conversion
- multi-chain routing
- treasury APIs
- wallet abstraction
- settlement orchestration
- compliance and reconciliation
Customers:
- PSPs, fintechs, marketplaces, global merchants, payroll/payout platforms
Revenue:
- spread, transaction fee, SaaS/API, treasury services

### Opportunity 2 — Cross-border B2B settlement
Status: A/B
Value:

- fewer correspondent-bank handoffs in some corridors

### Opportunity 4 — Merchant settlement
Status: A/B

Status: A/B

## Special cross-border-payment case path

The user should eventually be able to analyse:
- If a PSP uses USDC for backend settlement, where exactly is cost saved?
- Does stablecoin settlement really eliminate prefunding, or just relocate liquidity?
- What happens to FX spread?
- Who owns the client relationship: wallet, issuer, network, PSP or bank?
- How does stablecoin reserve income change economics?
- When does a bank prefer tokenised deposits over public stablecoins?
- Could stablecoins weaken correspondent banking, or will banks absorb the technology?
- Which part of WorldFirst / Airwallex / Payoneer / Ant International / Visa / Stripe style stacks is most exposed to change?
- Which opportunities are software-margin businesses and which remain balance-sheet businesses?

## Anti-hype rules

1. Never equate tokenisation with decentralisation.
2. Never equate stablecoin transfer speed with end-to-end payment speed.
3. Never ignore fiat on/off ramps.
4. Never ignore FX conversion.
5. Never ignore liquidity and prefunding.
6. Never ignore legal finality.
7. Never call a pilot “mass adoption”.
8. Never treat TVL or token market cap as equivalent to payments revenue.
9. Never assume public blockchains win over permissioned bank networks.
10. Never assume blockchain is necessary where a conventional database solves the problem better.
